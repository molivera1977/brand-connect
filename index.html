Section 01

8 Strategies to
Connect with Brands

Every strategy below is actionable from day one. Start where you are — not where you wish you were.

New to this? A "brand deal" simply means a company pays you — in money, free products, or both — to feature or promote them to your audience. Companies do this because people trust recommendations from creators they follow far more than traditional ads. Even small accounts with the right audience can be very attractive to brands.

Below are 8 proven ways to start making those connections happen, explained step by step.

01
🏗️
Build Your Foundation First

Before reaching out to any brand, your social media profile needs to look like a professional is running it. That means posting consistently (at least 3–4 times a week), having a clear niche (a specific topic you focus on — like fitness, cooking, travel, or fashion), a professional bio that explains who you are and what you post, and real interaction with your followers.

📖 What is "Engagement Rate"?
Your engagement rate is the percentage of your followers who actually interact with your posts through likes, comments, and shares. A creator with 1,000 followers and 100 interactions per post (10% engagement) is far more valuable to a brand than someone with 50,000 followers but only 100 interactions (0.2% engagement). Quality beats quantity every time.
Even 1,000 highly engaged followers can land deals with the right brand.
02
📍
Start Small & Local

Don't aim for Nike or Sephora on day one — those brands work with creators who have massive followings and a proven track record. Instead, start with small businesses and local brands in your area or niche. A local coffee shop, a small online clothing boutique, or a local gym are perfect starting points. They have smaller budgets but are much more willing to give new creators a chance.

📖 What is a "Micro-Influencer"?
A micro-influencer is someone with roughly 1,000 to 100,000 followers. Despite the smaller audience, studies show they often have higher engagement rates and more trust from their followers than mega-celebrities. Many brands specifically prefer working with micro-influencers because their recommendations feel more genuine and personal.
Local restaurants, boutiques, and service businesses are goldmines for beginners.
03
🎁
Create an Unprompted Gift Post

Here's a powerful trick: buy or use a product you genuinely love, create a high-quality post featuring it, tag the brand's official account in the post, and ask for absolutely nothing in return. This does two things — it gets you onto the brand's radar organically (they'll see the tag notification), and it gives them actual proof of what your content looks like featuring their product. It's a free audition.

📖 What does "tagging a brand" mean?
When you write a post on Instagram, TikTok, or other platforms, you can mention another account by typing @ followed by their username (e.g. @Nike). This sends them a notification that you've mentioned them, and they can click through to see your post. It puts your content directly in front of the people who matter at that brand.
Brands notice authentic content far more than promotional emails.
04
🔗
Use Affiliate Programs as a Backdoor

Affiliate programs let you earn a commission (a percentage of the sale) every time someone buys something through your unique link — with no need to pitch anyone. You sign up, get a special link, share it in your posts or bio, and earn money when your followers use it. Once a brand sees you're already generating real sales for them, the conversation about a paid partnership becomes much easier. You go from "unknown creator" to "proven performer."

📖 What is a "Commission"?
A commission is a percentage of a sale that you earn for sending a customer to a business. For example: if you share an Amazon link to a $50 backpack and someone buys it through your link, and Amazon pays a 5% commission, you earn $2.50. It doesn't cost the buyer anything extra — the store pays you out of their own profit margin.
Affiliate earnings are proof of performance — your best pitch tool.
05
📧
Pitch via Email, Not DM

When you're ready to reach out to a brand, skip the Instagram or TikTok DM (direct message). DMs get buried and rarely reach decision-makers. Instead, find the brand's PR (Public Relations) or marketing contact email — usually listed on their website under "Press" or "Contact" — and send a professional email. A well-written email with a media kit attached immediately separates you from the dozens of DMs they ignore every week.

📖 What is a "PR Contact" or "Cold Pitch"?
A PR contact is the person at a company who manages their public image and creator/media relationships — they're the ones who approve brand deals. A cold pitch means reaching out to someone who has never heard of you before. It's "cold" because there's no existing relationship — yet. The goal is to warm them up with a compelling, personalized message. See Section 04 of this guide for a full email template.
Subject line formula: "Collab idea — [Your Niche] + [Brand Name]"
06
📋
Build a Simple Media Kit

A media kit is your professional introduction document — think of it like a résumé, but for your social media presence. It's typically a clean one-page PDF that shows brands exactly who you are, how many people you reach, who your audience is, and what your content looks like. You don't need to be a designer to make one. The free tool Canva has ready-made media kit templates you can customize in minutes — just search "media kit" after signing up.

📖 What goes in a Media Kit?
Your media kit should include: a headshot and short bio, your follower counts on each platform, your average engagement rate, your audience demographics (age, gender, location — found in your platform's analytics), 3–5 examples of your best posts, and your contact email. You can find audience analytics in the "Insights" section of Instagram or TikTok's creator tools.
Include your audience demographics: age, location, interests.
07
💬
Engage Brands Before Pitching

Before you send a single pitch email, spend a few weeks actively engaging with the brand's own social media. Leave genuine, thoughtful comments on their posts (not just emojis — actual observations or compliments). Share their content to your stories. Respond when they post a question. This turns you from a complete stranger into a familiar face. When your pitch email arrives, they'll have seen your name before — and that makes a huge difference.

📖 Why does this work?
The person who reviews your pitch is the same person managing the brand's social media. When they see your name in an email, they may recognize it from your genuine comments. Recognition = credibility. It also shows you actually care about their brand, not just their marketing budget.
Genuine comments beat any cold outreach strategy.
08
🏪
Use Influencer Marketplaces

Influencer marketplaces are websites where brands actively search for creators to work with. Instead of you hunting for brands, you create a profile and brands come to you. Think of it like a job board, but brands are the ones hiring. You list your platforms, niche, follower count, and rates — and brands can browse, filter by category, and reach out to you directly. These are especially powerful for beginners because brands on these platforms specifically want to find new creators.

📖 What's the difference between "gifted" and "paid" deals?
A gifted deal means a brand sends you free products in exchange for a post — no money changes hands. A paid deal means the brand pays you a fee on top of (or instead of) free products. As a beginner, gifted deals are a normal starting point — they build your portfolio and your relationship with the brand, which can lead to paid deals later.
Complete your profile 100% — brands filter by niche, platform, and location.
Section 02

Understanding
Affiliate Programs

The most beginner-friendly way to start making money before brands come knocking — no follower minimum, no pitching required.

In plain English: an affiliate program is an arrangement where a company gives you a special tracking link. When one of your followers clicks that link and buys something, you automatically earn a percentage of the sale. The buyer pays the same price they would anyway — the store just shares a slice of their profit with you for sending the customer their way.

You don't need approval from any brand. You don't need to negotiate anything. You just sign up, grab your link, share it where your audience can see it, and the earnings come in automatically — including from posts you made months ago.

1
Join a Program
Visit an affiliate network's website (like Amazon Associates), fill out a short application, and get approved. Most programs are free to join and many approve you instantly with no follower requirement.
2
Get Your Unique Link
Once approved, you search for any product and generate a special link that's tied to your account. It looks like a normal link but has a hidden tracking code that tells the store "this sale came from you."
3
Share It with Your Audience
Post the link in your bio (using a tool like Linktree), include it in video descriptions, mention it in captions, or add it to your stories. Anywhere your audience can see and click it.
4
A Follower Buys
Someone clicks your link and purchases the product — within a set window (usually 24–90 days, depending on the program). The sale gets automatically attributed to you.
5
You Earn a Commission
Your earnings accumulate in your affiliate dashboard and get paid out monthly (usually via PayPal or direct deposit). Old content keeps earning — a video from 6 months ago can still generate income today.
Key Terms — Affiliate World

Words you'll need to know

Before diving into the platforms, here's a quick glossary of affiliate terms you'll see everywhere.

Cookie Window
The time period after someone clicks your link during which you still get credit for a purchase. Amazon's is 24 hours. Other programs offer 30, 60, or 90 days — longer is better for you.
Conversion Rate
The percentage of people who click your link and actually buy something. If 100 people click and 3 buy, your conversion rate is 3%. Higher-priced items often convert lower, but earn more per sale.
UGC
Stands for User-Generated Content — content (photos, videos, reviews) created by real people rather than a brand's own marketing team. Brands love UGC because it feels authentic. You can get paid to create UGC even without posting it to your own audience.
Payout Threshold
The minimum amount you need to earn before a program will pay you out. Amazon's is $10 for gift cards, $100 for direct deposit. Most programs pay monthly once you hit the threshold.
Deep Link
An affiliate link that goes directly to a specific product page rather than just the store's homepage. Always use deep links — they convert much better because the buyer lands exactly where they need to be.
FTC Disclosure: In the US, the law requires you to disclose when you're using affiliate links or getting paid/gifted by a brand. Simply adding "#ad", "#sponsored", or "affiliate link" to your post or video is enough — and it actually builds trust with your audience rather than hurting it.

Platforms &
Marketplaces

Every card links directly to the real platform. Pick what fits your niche and sign up today.

This section is organized into three categories. Affiliate Networks are platforms where you sign up to earn commissions on product sales — no brand approval needed, just a link to share. Influencer Marketplaces are directories where brands actively search for creators to hire — you create a profile and deals come to you. You don't have to use all of these. Pick one affiliate network and one marketplace to start. That's more than enough to get your first deal.

Section 04

The Perfect
Brand Pitch

Be specific. Be brief. Make it easy for them to say yes.

What is a "pitch"? A pitch is simply you reaching out to a brand and proposing a collaboration. It's no different from applying for a job — you're presenting yourself as someone worth investing in and suggesting a specific way you could work together.

The biggest mistake beginners make is sending a vague message like "I'd love to work together!" That tells the brand nothing. Instead, use the 5-part formula below. A well-structured pitch takes less than 5 minutes for a brand to read and gives them everything they need to say yes — without having to ask you a dozen follow-up questions.

P1
Who You Are
One sentence: your name, niche, and platform. Don't oversell — just state what you do and who your audience is.
P2
Why You Fit Their Brand
Show you've done your homework. Mention a specific product, campaign, or value of theirs that aligns with your content. Make it personal, not generic.
P3
A Specific Collaboration Idea
Propose one concrete thing. "A 60-second reel" beats "social content." Name the format, the platform, and ideally the timing or theme.
P4
Attach Your Media Kit
A one-page PDF with your stats, audience demographics, niche, platforms, and sample content. Keep it clean and visual — let it speak for you.
P5
A Soft Call-to-Action
Offer a 15-minute call or simply say you're happy to answer questions. Keep the door open. Don't pressure — give them room to respond naturally.
Sample Pitch Email
Subject: Collab Idea — Fitness Content + [Brand Name]

Hi [Name],

I'm [Your Name], a fitness creator on Instagram and TikTok with an audience of 4,200 active followers focused on at-home training and nutrition.

I've been using [Brand]'s resistance bands for three months and genuinely recommend them regularly. Your recent campaign around home gym setups aligns perfectly with the content I create.

I'd love to propose a 60-second reel featuring a full at-home workout using your bands — published in early August to coincide with back-to-school season when my audience is actively refreshing their fitness routines.

I've attached my media kit with full stats and audience demographics. Happy to jump on a quick 15-minute call if helpful — no pressure either way.

Thanks for your time,
[Your Name]
Section 05

Your Media Kit
Checklist

Build this before you pitch anyone. Use Canva.com — free media kit templates are ready instantly.

Think of your media kit as your résumé for brand deals. When you apply for a job, you send a résumé that summarizes your experience and skills. A media kit does the same thing, but for your social media presence. It's a clean, one-page PDF that answers every question a brand might have before agreeing to work with you.

You do not need to be a graphic designer. Go to canva.com, create a free account, search "influencer media kit," pick any template you like, swap in your own information and photos, and download it as a PDF. The whole thing takes about 30 minutes.

✓
Profile Photo & Bio
A professional headshot (good lighting, clean background — your phone camera is fine) and 2–3 sentences that describe your niche, who your audience is, and what makes your content unique. Example: "Fitness creator focused on at-home workouts for busy parents. 4,200 followers on Instagram and TikTok. Known for quick, equipment-free routines."
✓
Follower Count by Platform
List every platform you're active on — Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, etc. — with your current follower count next to each one. Be honest; brands can verify this instantly. Even if your numbers are small, showing multiple platforms demonstrates range.
✓
Engagement Rate
Calculate this yourself: add up your average likes and comments on recent posts, divide by your follower count, then multiply by 100. Example: 120 avg interactions ÷ 2,000 followers × 100 = 6% engagement rate. Anything above 3% is considered strong — highlight it prominently.
✓
Audience Demographics
This is data about your followers — their age range, gender split, and top countries or cities. You find this for free inside your platform's analytics: on Instagram go to Professional Dashboard → Audience. On TikTok go to Creator Tools → Analytics → Followers. Screenshot or copy these numbers directly.
✓
Content Niche & Style
Be as specific as possible about what you post and who it's for. "Sustainable fashion for budget-conscious college students" is far more powerful than "fashion content." The tighter your niche, the more valuable you are to the brands that fit that niche — even with a smaller following.
✓
Sample Content
Include 3–5 screenshots or thumbnails of your best posts. Choose content that would appeal to the type of brand you're pitching. No brand work yet? That's completely fine — use your best personal posts. The goal is to show quality and consistency, not necessarily sponsored experience.
✓
Contact Information
Include a professional email address. If your current email is something like partygirl2005@gmail.com, create a new one for your creator business — something like yourname.creates@gmail.com or hello@yourname.com. First impressions matter, and your email address is part of that.
✓
Services & Rates (Optional)
You can optionally list what you offer — sponsored Instagram posts, TikTok videos, YouTube integrations, Stories, UGC (photos/videos brands use in their own ads). For your first outreach, leaving rates off is fine — it opens the door for a conversation. Add them once you're more established and know your worth. Use Later's Rate Calculator to get a starting number.
Remember This
"You're not asking for a favor. You're offering a value exchange. Present yourself that way from day one."

The most successful influencers approach brands as equals — creative partners with something real to offer. Your audience's trust is valuable. Own it.
